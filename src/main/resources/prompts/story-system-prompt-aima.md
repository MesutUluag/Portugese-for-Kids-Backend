# Universal requirements (all D-type visa residency applications)
Every AIMA residency applicant must bring to their appointment:
- Valid passport with the D visa ("passaporte válido com o visto D") — Type D national long-stay visa, 4-month / 120-day window, 2 entries; verify all details match the passport exactly
- Portuguese NIF (Número de Identificação Fiscal)
- Portuguese bank account ("conta bancária em Portugal")
- AIMA application form ("formulário de pedido AIMA"), pre-filled but leave the "A preencher pelos serviços" box blank — that section is for AIMA staff only; errors are common so ensure every detail matches the passport exactly
- Copy of criminal record certificate ("cópia do certificado de registo criminal") — the same one submitted for the visa application; must be apostilled or authenticated; not required for minors under 16; an authenticated translation may be required even when the original was issued in English. Fill out a fresh "Declaração de Autorização" (Request for Criminal Record Enquiry, per article 12 of Visa law 84/2007) and sign it before the AIMA interview — this authorises AIMA to verify your criminal record in the Portuguese database
- Proof of entry in Portugal ("prova de entrada em Portugal") — in most cases the passport's virtual airport immigration stamp is sufficient; if entered by land (no Portuguese entry stamp), declare entry at a local police station and bring that declaration
- Proof of accommodation ("prova de alojamento"):
  - Rental: lease agreement ("contrato de arrendamento") + proof of registration with tax authorities ("comprovativo de registo nas Finanças")
  - Property purchase: proof of ownership and property registration ("certidão de registo predial")
  - Being hosted: Term of Responsibility ("Termo de Responsabilidade") filled, signed, and authenticated by a Portuguese notary
  - ⚠️ If you moved since visa approval, bring proof of accommodation for every address, not just the current one
- Declaration of stay ("Declaração de Alojamento"):
  - Rental: two declarations — one for the applicant, one signed and authenticated for the landlord
  - Property purchase: filled and signed by the applicant only
  - Being hosted: the authenticated Termo de Responsabilidade is sufficient (Declaration of Stay not required)
- Health insurance or SNS registration ("seguro de saúde ou inscrição no SNS") — AIMA may require a private health insurance policy valid for the entire residence period (minimum 2 years); the insurance requirement at the AIMA interview is a separate review from the visa interview. Note: SNS registration is accepted as proof but most Health Centres only allow registration after the TRC (residence card) is issued
- Request for Criminal Record Enquiry ("Declaração de Autorização") — must be filled and signed before the appointment; authorises AIMA to check Portuguese criminal records
- NISS (Número de Identificação de Segurança Social) — Social Security number, required to make social security contributions and access welfare benefits; since July 2026 AIMA may issue it automatically during the appointment; if not issued during the AIMA process, request it separately from Segurança Social

# Context: D7 Passive Income Visa
The D7 visa is for people with stable, recurring passive income (pension, rental income, dividends, investments) who wish to live in Portugal.

## Minimum income requirement
- Single applicant: €920/month (€11,040/year)
- Per accompanying spouse or adult: +€5,520/year
- Per child: +€3,312/year
- Bank account must show a deposited balance covering at least 12 months of the required minimum income

## Category-specific documents for D7
- Proof of passive income ("prova de rendimentos passivos"): tax returns, pension statements ("declaração de reforma"), dividend reports, or property rental contracts showing at least €920/month
- Bank statement confirming a balance equivalent to at least 12 months of the required income threshold

# AIMA appointment & post-approval residency timeline (all visa types)

## AIMA appointment (after visa approval)
- The residency visa is a Type D national long-stay visa: valid for Portugal only, 4-month / 120-day window, 2 entries; the appointment link or date is printed in the bottom-right corner of the visa sticker under "Agendamento / Appointment"
- In theory the AIMA appointment should be scheduled within the 120-day visa window; in practice the average wait is 7 months after visa approval
- If the visa has no appointment link in the bottom-right corner, no AIMA appointment was scheduled — the applicant must request one. Request via contactenos.aima.gov.pt/contact-form (primary channel) or geral@aima.gov.pt; you can only request once the visa is active and you have entered Portugal
- Submit the rescheduling / appointment request as soon as the visa is approved — earlier slots are not guaranteed and responses can take up to 3 months
- AIMA has 35 offices across Portugal (including Azores and Madeira): Braga, Porto, Aveiro, Coimbra, Lisboa, Setúbal, Évora, Faro, and others
- At the AIMA appointment the officer checks: lump-sum savings held in a Portuguese bank account ("poupança em conta portuguesa"), last 3 months of Portuguese bank statements ("extratos dos últimos três meses"), and the applicant's signature matching the passport
- AIMA can ask for additional documents it considers necessary — bring originals and organised copies of everything
- The physical residence card / TRC ("cartão de residência / título de residência") arrives 1.5–2 months after the appointment by registered post ("correio registado")
- AIMA announced a 60% capacity increase in H1 2026; wait times are expected to improve

## Post-approval residency timeline
Once the visa is approved and the applicant enters Portugal, the full journey to citizenship typically takes 7 to 10 years:
1. **Fly to Portugal** — must enter within the 120-day visa window
2. **AIMA appointment** — average wait is ~7 months after visa approval; converts the D visa into a TRC (Título de Residência / residence card)
3. **TRC granted** — initial residence permit issued; valid for 2 years
4. **Renewal of TRC** — after 2 years, renew the residence permit ("renovação da autorização de residência")
5. **Permanent residency application** ("pedido de autorização de residência permanente") — eligible after 3 years of legal residence with a valid TRC
6. **Citizenship application** ("pedido de nacionalidade portuguesa") — eligible after 2 or 5 years from the permanent residency grant; total journey is typically 7 to 10 years

# Other visa types — category-specific documents
- D1 Visa (subordinate worker / employed): declaration from employer; work contract; recent payslips and bank statements
- D2 Visa (entrepreneur): declaration of start of activity; proof of income and financial stability; certificate of no debt; business plan or proof of investment in Portugal
- D3 Visa (highly qualified activity): work contract or employer declaration meeting highly qualified regulation; CV; proof of higher education qualifications and/or specific training; recent payslips and bank statements
- D4 Visa (student, researcher, volunteer, internship): proof of enrolment in the Portuguese institute; proof of tuition payment and/or contributions for the specific programme (volunteering, traineeship, course)
- D6 Visa (family reunification): proof of residency of the family member in Portugal (residence permit, CRUE, or Cartão de Cidadão); proof of family relationship (marriage or birth certificate, apostilled and translated); proof of means of subsistence; authenticated photocopy of family member's passport
- D9 Visa (digital nomad / remote worker): proof of updated remote employment; payslips and bank statements for the last months
- G Visa (Golden Visa / ARI — Autorização de Residência para Atividade de Investimento): for those making a qualifying investment to gain residency in Portugal; proof of qualifying investment (real estate, capital transfer, job creation, etc.); proof of legal source of funds; valid passport; criminal record certificate; NIF; Portuguese bank account; proof of health insurance; minimum stay requirement (7 days/year in the first year, 14 days per subsequent 2-year period)

# Context: D9 Digital Nomad Visa
The D9 visa is for remote workers and freelancers whose employment or service contracts originate entirely outside Portugal.

## Minimum income requirement
- Minimum €3,680/month from remote work, verified across the last 3 months of payslips, invoices, and bank statements

## Category-specific documents for D9
- Employment contract ("contrato de trabalho") from a foreign employer confirming remote work authorization from Portugal, OR freelance agreements / service contracts ("contratos de prestação de serviços") with foreign clients
- Payslips ("recibos de vencimento") or invoices ("faturas") from the last 3 months proving income of at least €3,680/month
- Bank statements ("extratos bancários") for the last 3 months confirming income deposits
- Employer declaration ("declaração da entidade patronal") authorizing remote work from Portugal — required for salaried employees
- Tax residency document ("prova de residência fiscal") from the country of origin prior to relocating to Portugal
